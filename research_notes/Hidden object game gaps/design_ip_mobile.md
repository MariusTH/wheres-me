# Where's Me: Design, IP/Privacy, and Mobile Web Distribution

Research date: 2026-11-11. Project context: Godot 4.5 single-threaded web export on itch.io, aimed at mobile Safari and Chrome. It uses a selfie avatar via MediaPipe JS, a hide-and-seek share code, and a Museum mode with hue-shifted look-alikes. Note: the current Godot docs are for 4.7 stable, so 4.5.x is two minor versions behind.

## (a) What makes hidden-object search fun vs frustrating; tunable difficulty, fair hiding, hints, hider reward, accessibility

### Takeaway
Two factors set search difficulty, and both can be tuned. The first is how similar the target is to its distractors. The second is how mixed the distractors are among themselves. If the target differs in only one feature it "pops out", while targets defined by a combination of features have to be found item by item. Hidden Folks' lessons point the same way: tune difficulty through contrast and clue specificity, avoid punishing timers and failure states, let players tap freely with no penalty, and keep the interaction rules consistent. In Museum mode, look-alikes that differ only by hue are an accessibility hazard and need a second cue that is not colour.

### Cited Findings
**Visual search psychology**
- Feature Integration Theory (Treisman & Gelade, 1980): a target with a single unique feature gives flat search slopes, meaning more distractors do not slow the search ("pop-out"). A target defined by a conjunction of features gives steep slopes, because items must be inspected serially. — [Liesefeld & Müller review, Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13414-019-01819-z.pdf); [Treisman FIT paper (Utah mirror)](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~miriah/uncertainty/FeatureIntegrationTheoryOfAttention.pdf)
- Wolfe's Guided Search treats search as a continuum. Preattentive feature information "guides" serial attention, and conjunction search is often more efficient than strict FIT predicts. Search slopes are unreliable for classifying a search as serial or parallel. — [Liesefeld & Müller 2019](https://doi.org/10.3758%2Fs13414-019-01819-z); [Wolfe 2021 Guided Search 6.0](https://search.bwh.harvard.edu/new/pubs/Wolfe2021_GS6.pdf)
- Duncan & Humphreys (1989) similarity theory: search gets harder as target-distractor similarity rises, and also as distractors become more varied among themselves (lower distractor-distractor similarity). The effect is a continuum, not a set of categories. — [PMC3928141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3928141)
- A 2014 study that varied both factors independently found search was fastest with dissimilar targets and homogeneous distractors. With real-world objects, homogeneous distractors weaken the target-similarity effect because gaze lands on the target sooner. — [PMC3928141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3928141); [PMC3345177](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3345177)

**Hidden Folks (Adriaan de Jongh & Sylvain Tegroeg)**
- De Jongh: "In Where's Waldo? you're looking for specific graphics, whereas in Hidden Folks you are looking for a story." Clues sometimes point to whole areas and sometimes to specific objects placed "somewhere on the map that would make sense to people." — [PocketGamer interview](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/073054/hidden-folks-creator-adriann-de-jongh-gives-us-an-insight-into-this-quirky-little-puzzler/)
- De Jongh criticised the genre: "95% of hidden object games follow well-defined designs, containing the same level structures, timers, object highlights, game modes." — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/hidden-object-renaissance-or-not/)
- Difficulty was tuned by varying contrast around targets. The team avoided using contrast to guide the eye too strongly. — [Game Developer: Building thousands of tiny interactions into Hidden Folks](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/building-thousands-of-tiny-interactions-into-i-hidden-folks-i-)
- They kept a taxonomy of target types (in plain sight, behind known or unknown interactions, revealed by sound, hidden by timing or poor contrast) and of hint types (specific spot, small area, large area, particular indicator), and used it to vary challenge. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/building-thousands-of-tiny-interactions-into-i-hidden-folks-i-)
- Timed challenges were cut because replaying areas was "simply too frustrating for most players." The one remaining timed challenge was changed so the character waits instead of the player failing. The design goal was "taking your time and tapping everything with no penalty." — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/building-thousands-of-tiny-interactions-into-i-hidden-folks-i-)
- Interactivity creates expectations: one openable door made every door-like object seem openable. The team limited interactive objects and taught the rules in small tutorial areas. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/building-thousands-of-tiny-interactions-into-i-hidden-folks-i-)
- Multi-step puzzle targets were dropped because they were hard to read visually. Playtests were used to rewrite clues or jokes that didn't land. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/building-thousands-of-tiny-interactions-into-i-hidden-folks-i-)
- All sound is "100% mouth sounds", a cheap way to give feedback on every tap. — [PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/073054/hidden-folks-creator-adriann-de-jongh-gives-us-an-insight-into-this-quirky-little-puzzler/)
- Production lesson: keep the art style consistent across all assets. They bought the original cheap scanner again because a better one changed the look. — [Game Developer: illustrated walkthrough](https://gamedeveloper.com/production/here-s-an-illustrated-walkthrough-of-how-i-hidden-folks-i-was-made)

**Colour-blind accessibility**
- Game Accessibility Guidelines: essential information should never be conveyed by a fixed colour alone. — [gameaccessibilityguidelines.com](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/?p=3273)
- Examples: DOTS added shapes on top of colours in its colour-blind mode. Among Us was criticised for colour-only identity, with symbols or colour names (e.g. the ColorAdd system Uno uses) suggested instead. Zuma's Revenge has a "Color Blind Assist" option. — [Venngage](https://venngage.com/blog/color-blind-design/); [UX Collective Among Us case study](https://uxdesign.cc/the-importance-of-colorblind-friendly-design-case-study-among-us-dcd042c87b9?gi=b73ded9377a8); [IGDA GA-SIG](https://igda-gasig.org/?p=2386)
- Test with colour-blindness simulators and with colour-blind players. The Wong 8-colour palette is a common colour-blind-safe set. — [colorblindgames.com](https://colorblindgames.com/colorblind-gaming-101/); [pixelpaletteswap guide](https://pixelpaletteswap.pages.dev/guides/accessibility-high-contrast-palettes.html)

### Inferences
- **Difficulty knobs for the town map, based on Duncan & Humphreys and FIT:** (1) how many avatar features the player has to conjoin, e.g. shirt colour plus hat plus hair; (2) how similar NPC outfits are to the avatar; (3) how varied the NPCs are (a more varied crowd is harder); (4) local crowd density and contrast around the hide spot (the Hidden Folks contrast lever); (5) the visibility percentage (the existing >=70% rule). A hide-difficulty score could combine local density, mean feature similarity of NPCs within radius R, and contrast. That score could rate the hider, e.g. "Sneakiness: 4/5", and set the seeker's timer or point multiplier.
- **Fairness rule:** if the avatar is a unique combination of features, the search is hard but solvable serially. If an NPC is a near-exact clone of the avatar, it becomes guessing. Consider banning NPCs within a similarity threshold of the avatar near the hide spot. The >=70% visibility rule already stops pure occlusion hiding, which is good.
- **Hint ladder, from Hidden Folks hint types:** large area, then small area, then specific indicator. Each hint costs points or time rather than causing failure. Also consider a soft timer that adds a score penalty or grade instead of a hard fail, given Hidden Folks' finding that timers frustrated players. Wrong taps should give light, funny feedback, such as a mouth-sound "nope" from the NPC, rather than a penalty. A light penalty is optional to stop spam-tapping.
- **Rewarding the hider:** score inversely to seeker success. Report the time the seeker took and hints used back to the hider through a reply code. This creates a back-and-forth loop between friends.
- **Museum mode:** hue-only look-alikes fail for roughly 8% of men with colour vision deficiency (a commonly cited figure, not verified in this session). Vary pattern, shape, or accessory alongside hue, or offer an assist toggle that turns the hue difference into a pattern or icon difference. Check difficulty with a CVD simulator.

## (b) IP risks (Where's Waldo/Wally) and selfie privacy (GDPR, COPPA, BIPA)

### Takeaway
"Where's Wally?/Waldo?" is an actively licensed brand. Media and merchandising rights passed from Entertainment Rights through Classic Media to DreamWorks Animation and then NBCUniversal. Walker Books and Candlewick publish the books, and DreamWorks Distribution Limited has filed US trademark applications for "WHERE'S WALDO?". I found no public record of lawsuits against parody games, but parody is not a reliable defence against infringement. Mentioning Waldo nominatively in a description is lower risk. Copying the striped-shirt look, or a name built on "Where's ___" plus Waldo cues, raises risk. For the selfie feature, an August 2026 Seventh Circuit ruling (G.T. v. Samsung) helps purely on-device processing under BIPA, but consent and disclosure are still advised. COPPA risk depends on whether the game is child-directed.

### Cited Findings
**Ownership**
- Walker Books publishes the books in the UK. Candlewick Press (Walker Books Group, founded 1992) holds North American publishing rights and uses the title "Where's Waldo?". — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/childens/childrens-book-news/article/43202-where-s-waldo-at-walker-books-and-candlewick.html); [Candlewick (Waldo wiki)](https://waldo.fandom.com/wiki/Candlewick_Press)
- In January 2007 Entertainment Rights acquired "worldwide in perpetuity audio visual, licensing and merchandising, publishing, live events and digital rights to Where's Wally?". Martin Handford kept the right to keep creating the books. — [Brandora press release](https://brandora.de/news/N-2965)
- Entertainment Rights went into administration in 2009, and its catalogue went to Boomerang Media/Classic Media. DreamWorks Animation bought Classic Media in 2012 and renamed it DreamWorks Classics. NBCUniversal bought DreamWorks Animation in 2016. — [Wikipedia mirror: Where's Wally?](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/Where's_Wally); [Slice of SciFi 2012](https://www.sliceofscifi.com/2012/07/24/dreamworks-buys-he-man-casper-waldo/)
- USPTO: "WHERE'S WALDO?" applications in the name of DreamWorks Distribution Limited, e.g. serial 87790676 (eyewear, 2018) and serial 87980474 (published for opposition, January 2022). Justia's owner data is inconsistent, so verify on USPTO TSDR. — [Justia trademark 87980474](https://trademarks.justia.com/879/80/where-s-87980474.html); [Justia DreamWorks Distribution Ltd](https://trademarks.justia.com/owners/dreamworks-distribution-limited-3764490)
- The brand is still actively licensed, with new licensees and a 30th-anniversary food promotion using a Walker Books activity booklet. — [World Screen](https://worldscreen.com/whereas-wally-secures-new-licensees/)

**Enforcement and parody law**
- Searches found no reported Waldo/Wally lawsuit or cease-and-desist against games or parodies. — (negative result; searches on DreamWorks/Waldo litigation and Handford parody C&D)
- Parody does not automatically protect against trademark infringement. US law has a statutory parody defence for dilution claims but not for infringement claims. In Vans v. MSCHF, a "parody" shoe was held to infringe on appeal. After Jack Daniel's v. VIP (Bad Spaniels, 2023), parody used as a source identifier, i.e. as your own brand, gets less protection. — [Cowan Liebowitz & Latman alert](https://www.cll.com/media/news/861_Print_Pilot_-_Trademark_Law_Alert--U.S._Court_of_Appeals_Affirms-Shoe_E2_80_9CParody_E2_80_9D_was_a_Trademark_Infringement.pdf); [TRAMA on Jack Daniel's v Bad Spaniels](https://www.tramatm.com/en/blog/category/gastronomy/liability-of-parody-trademarks-the-case-of-jack-daniels-vs-bad-spaniels); [CMPR Law: dangers of parody trademarks](https://cmprlaw.com/the-dangers-of-parody-trademarks/)
- A Seuss-style satire was dropped after a cease-and-desist, showing that children's-brand owners send C&Ds over style imitation. — [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/articles/20120320/08024818171/cd-squashes-seuss-style-satire-where-did-the-idea-expression-dichotomy-go.shtml)
- Wordplay names carry risk too: the 2026 "John Lemon" beer dispute is a recent example. — [Food Manufacture](https://www.foodmanufacture.co.uk/Article/2026/06/01/john-lemon-beer-dispute-the-legal-risks-in-wordplay-branding/)
- Hidden Folks itself positions against Waldo ("looking for a story" rather than graphics), and shipped widely without IP trouble that I could find. — [PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/073054/hidden-folks-creator-adriann-de-jongh-gives-us-an-insight-into-this-quirky-little-puzzler/)

**Selfie and biometric privacy**
- In G.T. v. Samsung (7th Cir., August 2026), dismissal of a BIPA claim over face templates generated on the device was affirmed. "Possess/collect/capture/obtain" require the defendant to have some control over the data. The complaint failed because it did not allege the data left the device. — [Mayer Brown, Aug 20 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/seventh-circuit-holds-that-bipa-does-not-reach-biometric-data-that-remains-on-a-users-device); [WLF Legal Pulse PDF](https://www.wlf.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/090126Mancall-BitelEtAl_LP.pdf)
- Limits of that ruling: the court assumed, without deciding, that face geometry is a biometric identifier. It binds only federal courts in the Seventh Circuit. Commentators still recommend disclosures, consent, retention policies, and a way to turn the feature off and delete data. Earlier rulings were consistent: Hazlitt v. Apple held the user captures the data, and Bhavilai v. Microsoft (N.D. Ill. 2024) dismissed claims. A 2020 claim over Apple Photos face grouping did survive the pleading stage. — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/seventh-circuit-holds-that-bipa-does-not-reach-biometric-data-that-remains-on-a-users-device); [Hunton: BIPA suit proceeds against Apple](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/bipa-lawsuit-proceeds-against-apple-in-federal-court)
- COPPA: in the FTC's Recolor settlement, an app marketed to adults had a child-directed portion that let users import selfies and publish to a gallery. It was found in violation. — [WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-settles-coppa-action-against-coloring-book-for-adults.html); [Orrick InfoBytes](https://infobytes.orrick.com/2021-07-08/ftc-settles-app-violating-coppa/)
- Children's facial-scan suits against TikTok included COPPA allegations. — [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202011/lessons-from-tiktok-federal-and-state-law-implications-for-childrens-biometric-data-privacy)

### Inferences
- **Naming:** "Where's Me" shares the "Where's ___?" construction, and the pitch already references Waldo. "Where's" alone is a common phrase (compare "Where's My Water?" by Disney). Combined with a Waldo-style crowd scene and pitch, though, it invites comparison. Lowest risk: (1) never use a red-and-white striped shirt, bobble hat, round glasses, or a cane as default avatar or NPC items; (2) don't put "Waldo" or "Wally" in the title, tags, cover art, or SEO keywords; (3) if you mention it at all, use a plain comparative line such as "a seek-and-find game in the tradition of picture-book puzzles", or at most "inspired by classic seek-and-find books". Nominative "like Where's Waldo" in body text is lower risk than using it in the title or tags, but it adds nothing a brand owner would welcome. A free itch.io game is very unlikely to be sued. The realistic outcome is a C&D or itch.io takedown forcing a rename after you've built an audience, so rename early if that matters. This is not legal advice.
- Consider a distinct name that doesn't rely on the Waldo construction, which also helps searchability, or at least check "WHERE'S ME" on USPTO TSDR and EUIPO eSearch.
- **Selfie:** keep MediaPipe processing fully on-device (WASM/JS in the browser). Never upload the image or landmarks. Discard the raw photo after generating the avatar, and keep only stylised avatar parameters, e.g. colour indices, in the share code. Show a short just-in-time notice ("Your photo never leaves your device; we only keep the cartoon"). Offer a no-camera path. This matches the posture the Seventh Circuit found persuasive.
- **GDPR:** if no personal data reaches the developer, most GDPR obligations are minimal. Share codes containing only avatar parameters are probably not personal data. itch.io's own analytics is itch's responsibility. If the game is child-appealing, keep it non-child-directed in positioning, or avoid any data collection, to stay out of COPPA scope.

### Gaps
- I did not pull the USPTO TSDR or EUIPO records directly. Owner and status details come from Justia, which flagged inconsistent owner data.
- I found no trade-dress registration for the red-white striped outfit. Whether one exists is unverified. Character copyright, separate from trademark, likely protects Waldo's design regardless.
- I found no reported enforcement actions over Waldo/Wally specifically, which could mean none exist or that they were settled quietly.
- Not researched: GDPR Article 8 (age of consent, 13-16 by member state), UK Age Appropriate Design Code, and the Texas CUBI and Washington biometric laws.

## (c) Getting a Godot 4.5 web game onto phones, testing it, and when to go native; alternative portals and distribution

### Takeaway
Single-threaded export (the default since 4.3) is the right choice for iOS. The main iOS risks are memory-pressure crashes and reloads (iOS has no swap), audio-related crashes on some 4.x builds, and fullscreen. iPhone Safari doesn't support the Fullscreen API for canvas, so the fallback is "Add to Home Screen" with PWA meta tags. Test with Safari Web Inspector on a USB-connected iPhone or an Xcode simulator. CrazyGames accepts Godot single-threaded builds with a 20MB mobile homepage limit. Discord Activities can host a Godot web export. Go native only if web memory limits, fullscreen, or store discoverability become blockers.

### Cited Findings
**Godot web export facts (official docs, 4.7 stable page; applies to 4.3+)**
- Single-threaded export is the default since 4.3. It needs no special headers, runs on itch.io, Poki, and CrazyGames, and "works very well on macOS and iOS too", where threaded builds had problems. — [Godot docs: Exporting for the Web](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)
- Godot 4 web export is WebGL 2.0 only, using the Compatibility renderer. WebGPU, Forward+, and Mobile are unsupported. Safari has WebGL 2.0 issues that other browsers don't. — [Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)
- Audio defaults to Web Audio "Sample" playback since 4.3, which has no AudioEffects, reverb, doppler, or procedural audio. Stream mode restores the full audio system with more latency. Browsers block autoplay, so unlock audio with a tap or splash screen. — [Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)
- Fullscreen and pointer capture must be triggered from an input event (`_input` or `_unhandled_input`). Polling the Input singleton isn't enough. — [Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)
- The PWA option adds home-screen icons, display mode, and orientation settings, plus a service worker for offline play and cross-origin isolation headers. — [Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)
- Local serving: many features need HTTPS (localhost is exempt). Serve `.wasm` as `application/wasm` and use gzip or Brotli. `python serve.py --root .` is a testing server. — [Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)
- Godot 4.5 was released on 2025-09-18 with WebAssembly SIMD on by default for web. 4.5.1 and 4.5.2 are maintenance releases. — [Godot 4.5 release page](https://godotengine.org/releases/4.5/); [Cinevva news](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2025-09-18-godot-4-5-release)
- Godot's 4.3 web progress report: Apple devices had long-standing problems with Godot web exports, and these "fortunately disappear" when exporting single-threaded. Safari can reload the page with a "significant memory" warning. — [Godot blog: Progress report web export in 4.3](https://godotengine.org/article/progress-report-web-export-in-4-3/)

**Known iOS problems (mostly forum anecdotes)**
- On a 4.5-dev5 itch build, the game crashed or reloaded after 1-3 minutes of audio on iOS Safari and Chrome, but not on Android. 4.5 beta 2 and 3 reportedly fixed it, and converting sounds from Sample to Stream partly helped. — [Godot forum: sounds crash web game on mobile (4.5 dev5)](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/sounds-crash-my-web-game-on-mobile-browser-godot-4-5-dev5-html-export-on-itch/113237)

- A WebGL context loss and crash on iOS browsers with 4.3 is also reported. — [Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/webgl-context-loss-and-app-crash-in-godot-4-3-exported-web-on-ios-browsers/81024)
- Upgrading from 4.5.1 to 4.6 caused "Memory access out of bounds" errors on web, tracked as GitHub #115560. Reverting to 4.5 fixed it. — [Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/memory-access-out-of-bounds-on-web-export-migrating-4-5-1-4-6/131968)
- A 4.7 web build reportedly used about 1.2GB of RAM versus 274MB on Linux desktop. — [Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/4-7-stable-web-html5-consumes-much-more-ram-memory-compared-to-the-linux-desktop-x86-64-version-please-help-us/140351)
- All iOS browsers use WebKit, so switching to Chrome on iOS doesn't avoid Safari bugs. — [Godot forum: problems with iPhone](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/problems-with-iphone-when-exporting-to-html5/67937)

The workaround is to add `<meta name="apple-mobile-web-app-capable" content="yes">` to the Head Include and have users "Add to Home Screen". — [Godot forum: fullscreen on iPhone 4.3](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/full-screen-issue-with-web-export-in-godot-4-3-when-viewed-on-iphone/77672); [older thread](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/fullscreen-on-apple-products/9342)
- itch.io's "Mobile Friendly" embed option alone did not fix iPhone failures for one user. — [godotforums](https://godotforums.org/d/36339-html5-export-wont-run-in-mobile-browsers/14)

### Inferences
- **Stay on 4.5.x for now.** 4.6 has a reported web memory regression (#115560) and 4.7 reports high web RAM. Upgrade only after testing on a real iPhone.
- **iOS memory checklist:** keep the hand-drawn town as tiled or compressed textures rather than one huge PNG. Total texture memory matters more than download size, because textures decompress in RAM. Keep MediaPipe's WASM and model inside the budget: unload the face landmarker after avatar creation to free memory before loading the town. Use Stream playback or short OGGs for audio. Test on the oldest iPhone you can get, since low-RAM devices kill tabs first.
- **Audio:** put a "Tap to start" splash on the first screen. It unlocks audio and is also the right moment to request fullscreen on Android/Chrome. On iOS, show an "Add to Home Screen for fullscreen" tip, and add the apple-mobile-web-app-capable meta and a manifest via the PWA option. On itch.io, the iframe embed plus itch's own fullscreen button is the realistic route on iPhone. Make the viewport layout robust to Safari's dynamic toolbars.
- **Testing workflow (cheapest first):** (1) `serve.py` or `python -m http.server` on the LAN, opened from the phone via the Mac's IP. HTTP over LAN is not a secure context, so camera/getUserMedia for the selfie will fail. Use HTTPS via a tunnel (cloudflared `trycloudflare` quick tunnel or ngrok), or a self-signed certificate with mkcert. (2) Safari Web Inspector over USB or Wi-Fi to see console, memory, and WebGL errors. (3) Xcode iOS Simulator Safari for layout, though not for real memory or GPU behaviour. (4) A butler push to an itch.io draft or restricted page to test the real iframe. (5) BrowserStack for device coverage. Tunnels and mkcert are background knowledge; neither was verified in this session.
- **Selfie on itch:** camera access inside the itch iframe requires the iframe to allow the camera. Verify whether itch's embed grants `allow="camera"`; if not, the selfie only works in "open in new window" mode or on your own host. This is a likely high-impact gap to test.
- **When to go native:** if users hit memory crashes on iPhone, or you want home-screen/fullscreen without friction, push notifications, or store discoverability. For Android, Godot's native export plus Google Play internal testing is straightforward. For iOS, a native Godot export goes through TestFlight. Wrapping the web build in Capacitor or WKWebView keeps the WebKit memory limits and risks App Store Guideline 4.2 "minimum functionality" rejection, so native Godot export is better if you go to the stores (background knowledge, unverified). For a free share-code party game, web-first plus a Discord Activity is likely the better reach-per-effort.
- **Portals:** CrazyGames is feasible if the build stays under 20MB, but the MediaPipe model adds size. Poki is curated and needs SDK and ads integration. Both prefer games without a camera feature, so a camera-free build may be needed there.
- **Short-form marketing:** "Can you find me?" clips with a selfie avatar hidden in a crowd suit TikTok and Reels stitch and duet challenges. Post the share code in the caption so viewers can play the exact level. Speculative; no source found.
