# Turn every hide into a rematch

Where's Me sits in a gap nobody else has filled. The 2026 "hide from your friends" hits are real-time 3D server games, and the hand-drawn finders are single-player. No product found pairs **asynchronous "hide yourself, send a code"** play with a drawn crowd scene. On mobile, the existing setup (a single-threaded Godot web export pushed to itch by CI) is the right base. The work left is a disciplined test ladder that ends on real iPhones, plus three checks: whether the **camera works inside itch's iframe**, iOS **memory headroom** once MediaPipe and the town share RAM, and **audio stability**. Going native, or upgrading past 4.5.x, is not justified yet. On fun, the game is missing the layers that make the best finders and social games stick: Waldo-style side targets so a short search still has content, a Hidden Folks-style hint ladder and tap reactions in place of a hard timer, a "sneakiness" score that rewards the hider, and **reply codes that carry a running scoreboard** so each find prompts a hide back. A seeded daily hide with a spoiler-free emoji result would give the public sharing channel that private friend codes can't. The main risks are a C&D over Waldo resemblance (avoidable through naming and costume choices), selfie privacy (low if processing stays on-device), Museum mode's hue-only look-alikes (an accessibility failure as designed), and a genre crowded with $1–5 cat clones. A free, social game sidesteps that last risk if it leans hard on what clones lack.

## Web-first on 4.5.x, with a five-rung test ladder ending on a real iPhone

The export choice is already correct. Godot's docs say single-threaded export, the default since 4.3, needs no special headers, runs on itch, Poki and CrazyGames, and "works very well on macOS and iOS too" ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). The 4.3 web progress report says Apple's long-standing web-export problems "fortunately disappear" with single-threaded builds ([Godot blog](https://godotengine.org/article/progress-report-web-export-in-4-3/)). The bigger danger is upgrading. Moving from 4.5.1 to 4.6 produced "Memory access out of bounds" errors on web (GitHub #115560), fixed by reverting ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/memory-access-out-of-bounds-on-web-export-migrating-4-5-1-4-6/131968)). A 4.7 web build reportedly used **about 1.2GB of RAM against 274MB on desktop Linux** ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/4-7-stable-web-html5-consumes-much-more-ram-memory-compared-to-the-linux-desktop-x86-64-version-please-help-us/140351)). **Stay on 4.5.x** until a newer version has passed on a real iPhone.

Memory is the iOS failure mode to design around. iOS has no swap, so a tab that exceeds physical memory is killed ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/game-crashes-randomly-on-ios-devices-html-export/114043)), and Safari can reload the page with a "significant memory" warning ([Godot blog](https://godotengine.org/article/progress-report-web-export-in-4-3/)). Every iOS browser uses WebKit, so testing in Chrome on iPhone does not avoid Safari bugs ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/problems-with-iphone-when-exporting-to-html5/67937)). In practice: tile or compress the hand-drawn town instead of shipping one giant texture, since decoded textures (not download size) fill RAM. Unload the MediaPipe face landmarker and its WASM after the avatar is built and before the town loads. Test on the oldest, lowest-RAM iPhone available. Audio is the second suspect. A 4.5-dev5 itch build crashed or reloaded after 1–3 minutes of sound on iOS Safari and Chrome but not on Android. Later betas reportedly fixed it, and switching sounds from Sample to Stream playback partly helped ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/sounds-crash-my-web-game-on-mobile-browser-godot-4-5-dev5-html-export-on-itch/113237)). Browsers block autoplay, so a "Tap to start" splash should unlock audio ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). On Android the same tap can request fullscreen. Fullscreen requests must come from an input event, not from polling ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)). On iPhone, `window_set_mode(FULLSCREEN)` does nothing (GitHub #89220). The workaround is the `apple-mobile-web-app-capable` meta tag plus "Add to Home Screen" ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/full-screen-issue-with-web-export-in-godot-4-3-when-viewed-on-iphone/77672)). Inside itch, itch's own fullscreen button is the realistic route.

The test ladder runs cheapest first. **Rung one** is a local server reached from the phone over the LAN. Camera access needs a secure context (HTTPS, with localhost exempt) ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html)), so the selfie flow will fail over plain LAN HTTP. An HTTPS tunnel or a local certificate is needed for that path. This is the researchers' background knowledge, not verified. **Rung two** is Safari Web Inspector. Enable it on the iPhone under Settings > Safari > Advanced, connect over USB, then optionally switch to "Connect via Network" for wireless debugging ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/web-inspector/enabling-web-inspector/); [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/safari-developer-tools/develop-menu.md)). This is the only way to see console, WebGL and memory errors on the device itself. **Rung three** is the Xcode iOS Simulator, which always allows inspection and is good for layout. It does not reproduce real memory or GPU behaviour. **Rung four** is the existing CI butler push, pointed at a restricted or draft itch page so the real iframe is tested. Note that itch's "Mobile Friendly" toggle alone did not fix iPhone failures for one developer ([godotforums](https://godotforums.org/d/36339-html5-export-wont-run-in-mobile-browsers/14)). **Rung five** is BrowserStack's real-device iOS Safari for broader coverage ([BrowserStack](https://www.browserstack.com/guide/how-to-debug-on-iphone)). The top open question for rung four is whether **itch's embed iframe grants camera permission**. If it doesn't, the selfie only works in "open in new window" mode or on a self-hosted mirror. Nobody verified this, and it decides whether the headline feature works at all on the main distribution page.

Going native is premature. Native Godot export to TestFlight and Play internal testing would make sense only if memory crashes persist or home-screen and store presence become essential. Wrapping the web build in a WebView keeps WebKit's memory limits. Better reach-per-effort comes from two web extensions. A **Discord Activity** works: a Robo.js template already wires a Godot web export to Discord's embedded SDK, which runs on web, iOS and Android, provided threads are disabled ([robojs.dev](https://robojs.dev/templates/discord-activities/godot); [Discord SDK](https://github.com/discord/embedded-app-sdk)). **CrazyGames** accepts single-threaded Godot builds but caps the mobile homepage at **20MB** ([CrazyGames](https://docs.crazygames.com/requirements/technical/)), so a camera-free build without the MediaPipe model is probably needed there.

## The async hide-yourself niche is empty while the genre around it is crowded

The market splits into four tiers, and none of them is Where's Me. Premium hand-drawn finders win on craft. Hidden Folks holds **96% positive across 3,211 reviews** on the strength of 32 areas, 500+ interactions, 2,000+ mouth-made sound effects and clue text on every target ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/435400/Hidden_Folks/)). *Lost and Found Co.*, the 2026 breakout, sits near 97% positive with a Metascore around 90 ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2101390/lost_and_found_co); [The Geekly Grind](https://www.thegeeklygrind.com/all-posts/lost-and-found-co-review)). F2P mobile is June's Journey, which passed **$1B lifetime revenue** in November 2023 ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/woogas-junes-journey-hidden-object-game-surpasses-1b-in-revenue/)). Below both sits a flood of $1–5 Steam "hidden cats" reskins. *100 hidden cats* is estimated at about 41k copies and $32.6k gross ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/1587560/100_hidden_cats)), and Secret Cats alone has Pirate, Easter, Spooky and Ice Cream Day variants ([Datahumble](https://datahumble.com/secret-cats---pirates-3028440)). Demand is broad, but each clone earns little, and a free game doesn't have to compete on price.

The social tier shows the hook works. *MECCHA CHAMELEON*, a paint-yourself-to-blend-in hide-and-seek game, sold **more than 1M copies within days** of its June 2026 launch and peaked at about 132k concurrent players ([Galaxus](https://www.galaxus.at/en/page/painting-camouflaging-fearing-meccha-chameleon-becomes-a-steam-hit-43005); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4704690/MECCHA_CHAMELEON/)). Its complaints are bugs, unstable connections, a clumsy party system and no moderation ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/4704690/meccha_chameleon)). Where's Me's serverless async design avoids all of those by construction. The closest UGC finder, Hidden Through Time 2, has an "Architect" editor that reviewers call its main source of replay. Building levels is Steam-only, though, and its basic levels have little replay value of their own ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/hidden-through-time-2-myths-magic/review); [Softpedia](https://www.softpedia.com/reviews/games/playstation-5/hidden-through-time-2-myths-magic-review-537465.shtml)). In Where's Me, a "level" is a single placement, so creating one costs almost nothing. Mario Maker shows what cheap creation plus short shareable IDs can do: **2M courses in 11 days and 26M+ by 2021** ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2019/07/09/super-mario-maker-2-million-levels/); [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2021/05/super_mario_maker_2_players_have_now_uploaded_more_than_26_million_courses)). Searches found no mobile or web game offering friend-to-friend hiding through codes. That absence is a weak signal, not proof.

The steelman objection is Draw Something. It had the same "I make something personal, you solve it, you make one back" structure, peaked near **14.6M DAU in April 2012**, then lost 4M+ DAU within a month ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/draw-something-sheds-4m-daily-users-in-the-past-month)). Content exhaustion was one proposed cause, as players worked through its 900-word list ([The Week](https://theweek.com/articles/475789/draw-somethings-disastrous-month-3-theories-behind-slump)). BeReal fell from about 20M to about 10M DAU in a few months ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2023/02/22/bereal-may-be-on-the-out-users-have-nearly-halved-since-peak)). Where's Me should plan for a durable small-group habit, not a spike. Its defence against exhaustion is variety: more towns, seeded crowd randomisation and rotating scenes.

## What's missing: layers, gentle failure, hider reward, and a public channel

### Books and Hidden Folks show that one target is never enough

The bestselling seek-and-find series (Where's Wally sold more than 73M copies in 26 languages) layer several searches on each page. Each spread has a main target, a recurring cast (Wenda, Whitebeard, Odlaw, Woof), a per-scene checklist, and items held back to send readers through the book again ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where%27s_Wally%3F)). Pierre the Maze Detective adds a maze route and mystery challenges ([Hachette](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/hiro-kamigaki/pierre-the-maze-detective-the-hunt-for-the-maze-pyramid/9781510230620)). Right now a Where's Me search ends the moment the friend's avatar is found. A fixed **town checklist** (a dog with a balloon, a mime, a lost key) would give fast finders something more to do and give each town lasting value. A recurring town mascot hidden in every scene would add the "recurring cast" layer, and so would the Museum critter. De Jongh's framing helps here: "In Where's Waldo? you're looking for specific graphics, whereas in Hidden Folks you are looking for a story" ([PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/073054/hidden-folks-creator-adriann-de-jongh-gives-us-an-insight-into-this-quirky-little-puzzler/)). A one-line hider clue ("getting ice cream", "people-watching by the fountain") adds that story layer for one text field.

### The timer should grade, not fail

Hidden Folks cut its timed challenges because replaying areas was "simply too frustrating for most players". The design goal became "taking your time and tapping everything with no penalty" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/building-thousands-of-tiny-interactions-into-i-hidden-folks-i-)). Tiny Lands' reviewers praise its lack of a timer and misclick penalty ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1354910/tiny_lands)). Recurring player complaints across the genre are pixel hunting and the frustration of finding the last item. One player suggested hidden items periodically "shake" ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/6183196); [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/15630174)). Where's Me's timer should therefore set a **grade or score, not a fail state**, with a relaxed untimed toggle. Hidden Folks' hint taxonomy (large area, then small area, then specific indicator) maps onto an escalating **hint ladder** where each step costs points ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/building-thousands-of-tiny-interactions-into-i-hidden-folks-i-)). Wrong taps should get a funny reaction instead of a penalty: an NPC "nope" or a startled bird. Those tap reactions are the cheapest way to read as "Hidden Folks-like", and that game's whole soundscape is mouth sounds ([PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/073054/hidden-folks-creator-adriann-de-jongh-gives-us-an-insight-into-this-quirky-little-puzzler/)). One caution from the same team: one openable door made players expect every door to open, so keep interactions limited and consistent ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/building-thousands-of-tiny-interactions-into-i-hidden-folks-i-)).

### Visual-search science gives a sneakiness score and a fairness rule

Search gets harder as the target looks more like the distractors, and as the distractors look less like each other ([PMC3928141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3928141)). A target with one unique feature "pops out" whatever the crowd size, while a target defined by a combination of features (shirt plus hat plus hair) has to be found by checking items one at a time ([Liesefeld & Müller](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.3758/s13414-019-01819-z.pdf)). Hidden Folks tuned difficulty by varying contrast around targets ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/building-thousands-of-tiny-interactions-into-i-hidden-folks-i-)). Those knobs can be computed client-side when the hider places the avatar. Combine local crowd density, how similar nearby NPCs look to the avatar, how varied the crowd is, and local contrast into a **"Sneakiness" rating** that rewards the hider and scales the seeker's par time. The same computation enforces fairness. The ≥70% visibility rule already blocks hiding behind scenery. Add a minimum on-screen size, and bar near-clone NPCs within a radius of the hide spot, so a hard hide never turns into a guess. This is the hide-and-seek version of Wordle's word filtering: Wardle's partner cut 13,000 candidate words down to familiar ones because an obscure answer makes "you feel like you've been cheated by the game" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/josh-wardle-reflects-on-the-the-unconventional-road-to-wordle-s-success)).

### The share loop needs memory and a public face

Wordle's grid spread because players invented it, and it shows "how it went" without spoiling the answer. The once-a-day cadence turned it into a group-chat ritual ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/josh-wardle-reflects-on-the-the-unconventional-road-to-wordle-s-success)). NYT Games calls retention "the most important metric" and is building accounts specifically so players can keep their stats and streaks ([A Media Operator](https://www.amediaoperator.com/analysis/wordle-tv-show-new-york-times-games-jonathan-knight/)). Streak resets during the NYT migration caused real backlash ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/128799/wordles-move-to-the-nyt-website-has-reset-some-player-streaks)). Where's Me puzzles are private, so they lack Wordle's shared object. A **daily seeded hide** fixes that with no server: derive the town layout and hide spot from the date, and have everyone search the same one. A spoiler-free result line ("Where's Me #12 · 0:47 · 🔍🔍❌🔍✅") fits the public channel, while WM1 codes stay the private channel. Mario Maker's short IDs, and GeoGuessr challenge links that recipients play without an account ([TechWiser](https://techwiser.com/play-geoguessr-with-friends/)), point the same way: the recipient should be searching within seconds of pasting, with avatar creation deferred until after the result. The most important serverless upgrade is a **reply code that embeds the previous result**. Each hide-back then carries a running scoreboard ("You 2, Ola 1"), mirrored in a per-friend localStorage history. Gartic Phone grew through funny, stream-friendly output, with one session drawing 2.8M+ views ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/guides/what-is-gartic-phone-and-why-do-streamers-love-it-the-multiplayer-drawing-game-explained)). A post-find **reveal with a heatmap of the seeker's wrong taps** is Where's Me's screenshot-ready "look how blind you were" moment.

On transport, the current paste-a-code design is correct. itch does not pass the page's query string into the game iframe and offers "no official API" for it ([itch.io forum](https://itch.io/t/4128461/pass-the-query-parameters-down-to-iframe)). The URL fragment is neither read nor written there ([itch.io forum](https://itch.io/t/1466512/game-iframe-url-how-to-add-a-query-param)). Link-preview crawlers never see `#fragments` and don't run JavaScript ([Phabricator](https://phabricator.wikimedia.org/T431181)). So the share message text has to carry the hook ("Find me in Where's Me! [link] Code: WM1-…"), and the in-game paste should pull the WM1 token out of surrounding chat text with a regex. Whether itch's iframe allows `navigator.share` and clipboard-write is unverified, so keep the clipboard fallback.

## Prioritized feature list for a serverless web build

The ranking weighs viral-loop impact and risk reduction against effort. Every item runs client-side or encodes into the share code.

| # | Feature | Why (evidence) | Serverless implementation |
|---|---------|----------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | Verify camera in itch iframe; ship a no-camera avatar path and a privacy notice | The selfie feature may not work at all on the main page; on-device processing is the defensible privacy posture | Device test on a restricted itch page; fall back to "open in new window" or a self-hosted mirror |
| 2 | Non-colour cue in Museum look-alikes (pattern, shape, accessory) plus an assist toggle | Accessibility guidelines bar colour-only essential information | Look-alikes vary hue *and* one shape/pattern feature |
| 3 | Reply codes with embedded result and running scoreboard; "Hide back for [name]" on the result screen | Retaliation is the core viral engine; Draw Something structure | Add `{prev_time, taps, misses, hints, tally}` to the WM1 payload; per-friend history in localStorage |
| 4 | Soft timer (grade, not fail), relaxed mode, escalating hint ladder | Hidden Folks cut timers; last-item frustration | Hint 1 large area, hint 2 small area, hint 3 pulse; each costs score |
| 5 | Reveal screen with wrong-tap heatmap and a shareable emoji result line | Gartic-style funny artifacts; Wordle-style spoiler-free sharing | Record taps locally; render overlay; copy text line |
| 6 | Sneakiness score and fairness guard (min size, no near-clone NPCs near the spot) | Visual-search similarity theory; Wordle fairness lesson | Compute local density, similarity and contrast at placement time |
| 7 | Daily seeded hide with streak | Shared object for public sharing; streaks drive return | `seed = hash(date)` decides town, crowd and spot; streak in localStorage |
| 8 | Tap reactions and mouth-style sounds on scenery and NPCs | Most-quoted Hidden Folks praise | Limited, consistent set of animated props; short OGGs or Stream playback |
| 9 | Town checklist and recurring mascot per town | Book replay layers | Static per-town list; progress saved locally |
| 10 | Hider clue text (one line) | Hidden Folks "looking for a story" | Short string in the code (length-capped) |
| 11 | More towns and seeded crowd variation | Anti-"900 words" defence; reused-art complaints | Crowd shuffled from the code's seed |
| 12 | Discord Activity and camera-free CrazyGames build | Reach beyond itch at low effort | Robo.js Godot template; build under 20MB |

## Risks: a rename now costs less than a takedown later

**Waldo IP.** The brand is actively licensed. Audiovisual, merchandising and digital rights moved from Entertainment Rights through Classic Media to DreamWorks and then NBCUniversal ([Brandora](https://brandora.de/news/N-2965); [Slice of SciFi](https://www.sliceofscifi.com/2012/07/24/dreamworks-buys-he-man-casper-waldo/)). DreamWorks Distribution Limited has filed "WHERE'S WALDO?" trademark applications ([Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/879/80/where-s-87980474.html)). Searches found no lawsuits against parody games, but parody is no defence to US trademark infringement. In *Vans v. MSCHF* a "parody" shoe was held to infringe ([CLL](https://www.cll.com/media/news/861_Print_Pilot_-_Trademark_Law_Alert--U.S._Court_of_Appeals_Affirms-Shoe_E2_80_9CParody_E2_80_9D_was_a_Trademark_Infringement.pdf)), and children's-brand owners do send C&Ds over style imitation ([Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/articles/20120320/08024818171/cd-squashes-seuss-style-satire-where-did-the-idea-expression-dichotomy-go.shtml)). A lawsuit against a free itch game is unlikely. The realistic downside is a C&D or itch takedown after an audience exists. Keep striped red-and-white shirts, bobble hats, round glasses and canes out of the default items. Keep "Waldo/Wally" out of the title, tags and cover. Check "WHERE'S ME" on USPTO TSDR and EUIPO before investing in the name. Hidden Folks positioned itself explicitly *against* Waldo and shipped without trouble ([PocketGamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/073054/hidden-folks-creator-adriann-de-jongh-gives-us-an-insight-into-this-quirky-little-puzzler/)). This is not legal advice.

**Selfie privacy.** In *G.T. v. Samsung* (7th Cir., August 2026), the court affirmed dismissal of a BIPA claim over face data that never left the device ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/seventh-circuit-holds-that-bipa-does-not-reach-biometric-data-that-remains-on-a-users-device)). That supports in-browser MediaPipe processing. It binds only one circuit, though, and commentators still advise disclosure, consent and an off switch. The posture to hold: never upload the image or landmarks, discard the photo once the avatar is made, encode only stylised parameters in the code, show a just-in-time "your photo never leaves your device" line, and keep a no-camera path. COPPA is the sharper edge. The FTC's Recolor settlement hit an adult-marketed app because a child-directed portion let users import and publish selfies ([WSGR](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/ftc-settles-coppa-action-against-coloring-book-for-adults.html)). A cute hide-and-seek game appeals to children, so position it as general-audience and collect nothing. Note that adding an analytics ping to measure the viral K-factor would erode this zero-data stance.

**Colour-only look-alikes.** The Game Accessibility Guidelines say essential information must never rely on colour alone ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/?p=3273)). Among Us drew criticism for colour-only identity, while DOTS added shapes in its colour-blind mode ([UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/the-importance-of-colorblind-friendly-design-case-study-among-us-dcd042c87b9?gi=b73ded9377a8); [Venngage](https://venngage.com/blog/color-blind-design/)). As specified, hue-shifted Museum mode is unfair to colour-blind players. Add a shape or pattern difference and check it in a CVD simulator.

**Genre crowding.** Steam's cat-finder wave and 2026's tidy-up and finder hybrids make "another seek-and-find" invisible ([wasdland](https://www.wasdland.com/games/hidden-object/)). itch discovery relies on tags and devlogs, and site search favours exact title matches ([itch.io blog](https://itch.io/blog/88453/tips-and-tricks-to-help-your-itchio-launch); [itch.io forum](https://itch.io/post/7837802/view-in-topic)), which is one more argument for a distinctive name. Where's Me's differentiation is social, not visual. If the reply loop and the reveal are weak, it competes on art with Hidden Folks and loses. Sustained viral coefficients above 1 are rare in games, and there is no reliable public benchmark ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/viral-coefficient-organic-uplift-mobile-games)). Without a backend, K can't be measured directly anyway.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the product. Where's Me is less a hidden-object game with a social feature than a **turn-based messaging game whose turns happen to be hides**, closer to Draw Something and GamePigeon than to Hidden Folks. That changes the priorities: recipient time-to-search, reply-code memory and a funny reveal matter more than more art. The art matters mainly as the defence against content exhaustion. Two unknowns can each break a headline feature and cost an afternoon to resolve: camera permission inside itch's iframe, and iOS memory with MediaPipe and the town both loaded. Settle both on a real iPhone before building anything on this list. Data is also thin in several places: no usage numbers for HTT2's community levels, no K-factor or retention benchmarks for async friend games, and no evidence that selfie avatars lift sharing rather than just recognisability. Treat the selfie as delight, not as the growth engine, and make the no-camera path just as good.
