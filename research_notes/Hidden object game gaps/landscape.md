# Hidden-object / seek-and-find landscape (as of Nov 2026)

Scope: books, premium indie finders, cheap Steam "find the cats" games, F2P mobile HOGs, and social hide-and-seek, as context for "Where's Me" (a free web game where friends hide an avatar for each other). Research date: 2026-11-11. Third-party sales estimates (Raijin, Vaporlens, Datahumble, SteamSpy) are labelled as estimates.

Corrections to the brief:
- **Finding Frankie** (SUPERLOU) is a first-person horror parkour game-show game, not a hidden-object game. It has about 1.5–1.9k Steam reviews, rated Very Positive. — [wasdland](https://www.wasdland.com/game/finding-frankie-2597970/); [gamegeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/games/finding-frankie-2597970)
- **Pierre the Maze Detective** is published by Hachette (Laurence King imprint lineage), not Usborne. — [Hachette](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/hiro-kamigaki/pierre-the-maze-detective-the-hunt-for-the-maze-pyramid/9781510230620)
- I did not research Isle of Arrows. As far as I know it is a tile-placing tower-defence game rather than a finder, but I have not verified this.

## Books: what makes the best seek-and-find books replayable?

### Takeaway
The top books get replay value by putting several layers of searching on each page: one main target, recurring side characters, a checklist of extra items for every scene, and items that can only be found by going back through the book after the last page. Pierre adds a maze route and a mystery story on top of the search. Where's Wally has sold roughly 72–75M copies, which shows how big the "find one character in a crowd" format is.

### Cited Findings
- Where's Wally sales: more than 73M copies sold worldwide as of 2007. Publisher listings give 72M or 75M. The series has been translated into 26 languages and published in more than 50 countries. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where%27s_Wally%3F); [Pan Macmillan SA](https://www.panmacmillan.co.za/books/wheres-wally/9781406305890); [Walker Books AU](https://walkerbooks.com.au/book-works/wheres-wally)
- Early sales: the first four Wally books sold more than 18M copies in the series' first four years. — [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/fun/games/14-facts-about-wheres-waldo)
- Wally's replay layers:
  - each scene has a checklist of "interesting things or people to find";
  - a recurring cast to find on every page: Wenda, Wizard Whitebeard, Odlaw, and Woof (in early books only Woof's tail shows);
  - book-specific items, some held back until the end so the reader has to go through the book again.
  — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where%27s_Wally%3F)
- Tiny distinguishing details are part of the design: Whitebeard is "often" found by spotting his long beard. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where%27s_Wally%3F)
- Wally controversy: a topless beachgoer in one scene caused the book to be pulled from some US shelves. It appeared on the ALA's list of the 100 most-challenged books of the 1990s. This is a reminder that crowded scenes need moderating. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where%27s_Wally%3F)
- Pierre the Maze Detective (Hiro Kamigaki / IC4 Design, ages 7+): readers trace a maze route, search for hidden objects, spot clues and solve "extra mystery challenges". An in-book "scrap of paper" lists the items to find. The newest title, *The Hunt for the Maze Pyramid*, came out in 2025. — [Hachette](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/hiro-kamigaki/pierre-the-maze-detective-the-hunt-for-the-maze-pyramid/9781510230620); [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/58848144)
- A reader review says Pierre feels less "male orientated" than Wally, so the two are often compared. — [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/58848144)

### Inferences
- The book formula has four replay layers:
  1. a main target;
  2. a recurring cast hidden on every page;
  3. a checklist per scene;
  4. a reason to go back through the book (end-of-book reveals).
- Where's Me could use the same layers. A shared town could carry a fixed "town checklist" (for example a dog with a balloon) alongside the friend's hidden avatar, so a search that is over quickly still leaves something to do.
- Pierre's story and route layer suggests that a one-line "story" clue from the hider ("I'm getting ice cream") could add flavour, much like Hidden Folks' clue text.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced interior details or current sales for I Spy (Marzollo/Wick), Usborne puzzle books (e.g. Puzzle Adventures, 1001 Things to Spot), Richard Scarry (Goldbug), or Where's Spot. Where's Spot is lift-the-flap rather than crowd-search, and I have not verified this.
- I found no data on 2024–2026 seek-and-find book bestsellers.

## Games: notable products, monetisation, signals, praise and complaints

### Takeaway
The premium indie benchmarks are Hidden Folks (2017, 96% positive on about 3.2k reviews), Hidden Through Time 1 and 2, Tiny Lands, and *Lost and Found Co.*, the 2026 breakout (about 97% positive on 2k+ reviews, Metascore around 90). F2P mobile is dominated by June's Journey, which passed $1B lifetime revenue in Nov 2023. Steam is flooded with $1–5 "hidden cats" games that each sell a few thousand to tens of thousands of copies. Praise centres on hand-drawn interactive scenes, sound, and clue text. Complaints centre on pixel hunting, the frustration of finding the last item, short length, and reused art.

### Cited Findings

**Hidden Folks** (Adriaan de Jongh & Sylvain Tegroeg, 15 Feb 2017; PC/Mac/Linux, iOS, Apple TV; premium)
- Steam reviews: Overwhelmingly Positive, 96% of 3,211 English reviews. Recent reviews: 80% of 15. — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/435400/Hidden_Folks/)
- Content:
  - 32 hand-drawn areas with 300+ targets;
  - 500+ interactions (tent flaps, bushes, doors);
  - 2,000+ mouth-made sound effects;
  - a target strip where clicking a target shows a text hint;
  - finding enough targets unlocks the next area;
  - normal, sepia and night visual modes;
  - 23 languages.
  — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/435400/Hidden_Folks/)
- Monetisation: a premium base game plus a small cosmetic "Mouth Sounds Pack" DLC. — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/435400/Hidden_Folks/)
- Tegroeg drew more than 6,000 art pieces. Apple named the game among the App Store's best of 2017. — [Apple story](https://apps.apple.com/us/story/id1279820280)
- Press framed it as "a cute and whimsical Where's Waldo for the digital age". — [iMore](https://www.imore.com/hidden-folks-review)
- Sales figures: none found. SteamSpy's page for the developer does not appear to include Hidden Folks. — [SteamSpy](https://www.steamspy.com/dev/Adriaan+de+Jongh)

**Hidden Through Time 1 & 2** (Rogueside; HTT1 released 12 Mar 2020; HTT2: Myths & Magic on PC, consoles and mobile)

### Inferences
- Several sales signals point to Hidden Folks-style quality working as a premium product, though none of them is a hard number:
  - review counts are modest (about 3k for Hidden Folks and about 2k for Lost and Found Co.);
  - review scores are top-tier;
  - Hidden Folks and Lost and Found Co. are bundled together.
- The cheap cat clones show that demand is broad, but each title earns little. A free web game avoids competing on price altogether.
- The features that set a game apart are interactivity and sound: Hidden Folks' 500+ interactions and 2,000+ mouth sounds are the most quoted features. If Where's Me's town has tap reactions (doors, birds), that reads as "Hidden Folks-like" to players.
- Tiny Lands shows that "no timer, no hints" can work for a relaxing audience. Where's Me uses a timer, so it should probably offer an untimed or relaxed option.

## Which games include user-generated levels or friend-vs-friend hiding, and how well do those features do?

### Takeaway
Among dedicated finders, Hidden Through Time 2's Architect mode is the clearest example of UGC. Reviewers praise it, but its build tools are limited to Steam, and I found no usage figures. The big 2026 success in "hide from your friends" is real-time multiplayer: *MECCHA CHAMELEON* (Jun 2026) sold more than 1M copies within days and peaked at about 132k concurrent players. I found no asynchronous "hide yourself, send a code, friend searches a drawn scene" product, which is a gap Where's Me could fill.

### Cited Findings
- HTT2 Architect mode:
  - publishes levels online;
  - reviewers call community levels the main source of replay value;
  - designing is Steam-only, while playing community levels works on more platforms.
  — [gamebrain](https://gamebrain.co/game/hidden-through-time-2-myths-magic); [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/hidden-through-time-2-myths-magic/review); [Softpedia](https://www.softpedia.com/reviews/games/playstation-5/hidden-through-time-2-myths-magic-review-537465.shtml)
- *MECCHA CHAMELEON* (Jun 2026, $5.99): hide-and-seek where you paint yourself to blend in.
  - Sales: more than 1M within days.
  - Players: about 132k concurrent on release day (SteamDB, as reported).
  - Reviews: about 35k English reviews, Very Positive.
  - Complaints: bugs, unstable connections, an unfriendly party system, no moderation; it "shines only with friends".
  — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4704690/MECCHA_CHAMELEON/); [Galaxus](https://www.galaxus.at/en/page/painting-camouflaging-fearing-meccha-chameleon-becomes-a-steam-hit-43005); [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/4704690/meccha_chameleon)
- *SCRIBBLE HUNT* (22 Jul 2026, €4.15): 2–10 player online hide-and-seek with painted disguises. — [wasdland](https://www.wasdland.com/games/hidden-object/)

### Inferences
- Hiding yourself from a friend is a proven 2026 hook (MECCHA CHAMELEON), but the current hits are synchronous 3D games with servers. Where's Me's asynchronous share-code model avoids their main complaints: connection problems, party systems and moderation.
- The avatar-camouflage idea maps directly onto Where's Me. A rule that the avatar must be at least 70% visible is the counterpart to the fairness complaints in real-time camo games.
- HTT2 shows that UGC levels extend a finder's life, but the people building levels are a small group. Where's Me's "level" is just one placement, which lowers the effort of creating to nearly nothing.

### Gaps
- No public usage numbers for HTT2 community levels: how many are created or played.
- Not checked: whether any mobile finder offers friend-to-friend hiding through links or codes. No examples turned up in searches.

## Common player complaints and praised features

### Takeaway
Complaints: pixel hunting (targets too small or blending into the background), trouble finding the last item, unclear target identity, short length, and reused art or animations. Praise: hand-drawn density, interactive scenery, playful sound, text hints, a relaxed no-timer option, and level editors.

### Cited Findings
- Pixel hunting:
  - itch.io players call tiny, background-coloured targets "a literal pixel hunt";
  - finding the "last hidden object" is a recurring pain point;
  - one player suggested hidden items periodically "shake" as a gentle cue.
  — [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/6183196); [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/15630174)
- TV Tropes classes pixel hunting as "fake difficulty" in graphical adventures. — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/PixelHunt)
- Repetition: a Big Fish reviewer complained that the same collectibles appear across a developer's games. — [Big Fish](https://bigfishgames.com/us/en/games/17118/searchscape/?lang=en)
- Art reuse and lack of life: HTT2 was criticised for lacking unique animations, and for basic levels with little replay value. — [CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/hidden-through-time-2-myths-magic); [Softpedia](https://www.softpedia.com/reviews/games/playstation-5/hidden-through-time-2-myths-magic-review-537465.shtml)
- Bad difficulty: Gamezebo said the Waldo game's difficulty came from "poor choices in game design". — [Gamezebo](https://www.gamezebo.com/reviews/wheres-waldo-the-fantastic-journey-review/)
- Praise for Hidden Folks: interactions, mouth-sound audio, and clue text on each target. — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/435400/Hidden_Folks/)
- Praise for Tiny Lands: no timer and no misclick penalty. — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1354910/tiny_lands)

### Inferences
Design implications for Where's Me:
- Enforce the ≥70% visibility rule plus a minimum on-screen size for the avatar, so it never becomes a pixel hunt.
- Offer a timed hint that escalates (a shake, then a pulse, then a highlighted region) to deal with the last-item frustration.
- Let the hider add a short clue text, like Hidden Folks' hints.
- Add tap reactions and sounds to the scene.
- Rotate or vary scenes so players do not complain about reused art.
- For museum mode, colour-shifted look-alikes risk the "unfair" complaint. Make sure the differences can be seen at phone size and are not colour-only, so colour-blind players can play.
